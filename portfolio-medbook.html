About

MedBook is a clinic appointment scheduler with three role-based experiences behind an ASP.NET Identity login. The institution view sees the whole clinic — every provider's schedule, every patient, provider management. Doctors sign in to just their own world: their day's schedule, their availability editor, booking limited to their own calendar. Patients get a personal portal: only their appointments, self-service booking, rescheduling, and cancellation. The interesting part isn't the CRUD — it's the booking rules. Every appointment must fall entirely inside one of the provider's availability blocks, can't overlap the provider's or the patient's other active appointments, can't be booked in the past, and is limited to standard visit lengths. Try to double-book a slot and the form tells you exactly which existing appointment is in the way.

Architecturally it's a deliberate exercise in keeping MVC controllers thin. All of the scheduling logic lives in a dedicated SchedulingService behind an interface, injected via dependency injection, so every write path funnels through one tested gatekeeper. Authorization is enforced server-side, not just hidden in the UI: doctor and patient accounts are linked to their clinic records, ownership is checked on every read and write, and posted form values can't book onto another provider's calendar or on another patient's behalf. Private data is protected at rest too — passwords are PBKDF2-hashed by Identity, and the whole SQLite database is SQLCipher-encrypted, so patient records are unreadable without the key. An xUnit suite runs the real service against an in-memory SQLite database with a fixed clock, covering each rule: availability boundaries, provider and patient conflicts, back-to-back bookings, cancelled slots freeing up, and edits that shouldn't collide with themselves.

The app is intentionally zero-setup: EF Core migrations apply automatically at startup and the database seeds itself with four providers, a dozen patients, two weeks of realistic appointments, and demo institution/doctor/patient logins — clone it, dotnet run, and sign in to any side of the clinic.
