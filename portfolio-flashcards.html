About

Recall is an Anki-style flashcard app built around the SM-2 spaced-repetition algorithm, aimed at anyone who has to move a mountain of facts into long-term memory: study only what is about to slip, right before it slips. Decks and cards get full CRUD with markdown-lite formatting, study sessions flip to reveal and grade with Again / Hard / Good / Easy — each button previewing the interval it would grant — and failed cards cycle back into the current session. A due queue introduces new cards under a configurable daily limit, a stats dashboard tracks due counts, learning stages, a 30-day review chart, and retention, and studying is keyboard-first: Space to flip, 1–4 to grade. Everything runs in the browser against localStorage, with a seeded demo deck of medical mnemonics and lab values.

The scheduler is the engineering centerpiece: SM-2 lives in src/lib/sm2.ts as a pure, documented module with no dependencies on the UI or storage layers. Every card carries an ease factor (starting at 2.5, floored at 1.3), an interval, and a repetition count. Successful reviews walk the interval up — 1 day, 6 days, then previous interval times ease — while every grade nudges ease up or down, and lapses reset the card to a 1-day interval with a lowered ease so relearned material climbs back more cautiously. The 1.3 floor guarantees intervals always grow, so no card can get stuck shrinking forever.

Because the scheduler is pure, its behavior is pinned down by a 23-test Vitest suite: interval progression, ease-factor adjustments and the floor, lapse handling, the mapping from the four grading buttons onto SM-2's 0–5 quality scale, and input validation. Decks round-trip as JSON including scheduling state, so exports double as full backups.
